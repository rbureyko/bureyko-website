Mortgage rates get a lot of attention, and it's easy to see why. Your rate affects your payment, and over the years, it adds up to a lot of money.

But rates can also be confusing. Why did your friend get a different rate than you? Why is the rate on the bank's website different from the one you were offered? Let's sort it out.

## What moves mortgage rates?

A few big-picture things shape rates for everyone:

- **The Bank of Canada.** When it changes its policy interest rate, lenders' prime rates usually follow. That's what moves variable rates.
- **The bond market.** Fixed rates tend to follow longer-term borrowing costs, which move with the bond market.
- **Lenders' own costs and competition.** Every lender sets its own rates, so they can differ from one lender to the next.

## Why your rate might be different from someone else's

The rate you're offered also depends on you and the mortgage you choose. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada lists things like:

- The length of your term
- Whether you choose fixed or variable
- Your credit history
- Whether you're self-employed
- Whether you qualify for a discount off the lender's posted rate
- The type of lender you choose, and the specific lender

That's why two people can walk into the same bank on the same day and be offered different rates.

## Posted rates vs the rate you actually get

Lenders publish a "posted" rate. But many borrowers get a discounted rate that's lower than posted.

The posted rate still matters, though. Some lenders use it when they calculate your prepayment penalty if you break your mortgage early. So it's worth understanding how your lender does that math.

## Why the lowest rate isn't always the best deal

A low rate is great. But it's only one part of the picture. Sometimes a rock-bottom rate comes with trade-offs, like:

- **Bigger penalties** if you need to break your mortgage early
- **Fewer prepayment options** for paying it down faster
- **Less flexibility** if you need to move, refinance, or switch lenders later

If there's a decent chance your life could change in the next few years (a new job, a growing family, a move), flexibility might be worth more than a slightly lower rate.

## A few ways to put yourself in a better position

1. **Look after your credit.** A stronger credit history can help you get a better rate.
2. **Shop around.** The FCAC notes that comparing lenders could save you thousands of dollars.
3. **Shop smart.** If you're comparing mortgage quotes, try to do it within about two weeks. The credit bureaus treat those checks as one inquiry.
4. **Don't just accept your renewal offer.** You can negotiate, or move to another lender.
