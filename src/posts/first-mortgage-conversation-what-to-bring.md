Many first-time buyers wait to call a mortgage broker until they feel "ready." In practice, an early conversation is one of the best ways to get ready. You don't need a finished file. You need a rough picture.

## A rough picture is enough

For a first conversation, it helps to know approximately:

- **Your income.** Your pay, and whether it's salaried, hourly, commission, or self-employed.
- **Your down payment.** About how much you have, and where it's coming from.
- **Your current debts.** Car loans, student loans, credit cards, lines of credit.
- **Your timeline.** Are you buying this spring, or in a year or two?

Estimates are fine. We'll help you work out what needs more detail.

## What can wait

You don't need to gather pay stubs, tax documents, or bank statements before the first call. Which documents matter depends on your situation, and we'll explain exactly what to collect once we understand it.

Please don't send banking information, your SIN, or identity documents by general email or text. When it's time, we'll use a secure application.

## Questions worth asking

Bring your questions too. A few common ones:

- What price range makes sense for my budget?
- What does a pre-approval do, and what doesn't it guarantee?
- What closing costs should I plan for?
- How should I time things with my realtor?

## The goal of the first conversation

By the end, you should know where you stand, what needs attention, and what your next step is. That might be starting a pre-approval, or it might be a few months of preparation first.
