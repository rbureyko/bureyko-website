When you need a mortgage, you've got two main paths. You can go straight to a bank or credit union, or you can work with a mortgage broker.

Both can be great options. But a lot of people aren't quite sure what a broker does, or how we get paid. So let's clear that up, honestly and simply.

## What does a mortgage broker do?

A mortgage broker connects you with mortgage lenders. Instead of offering just one lender's products, a broker can compare options from a range of lenders, like banks, credit unions, and other lenders.

In practice, that usually means a broker will:

- Get to know your situation: your income, credit, down payment, and goals
- Look at options across different lenders
- Explain the differences in plain language, including the fine print
- Help you gather your documents and submit your application
- Stay with you through closing day and beyond. At Bureyko Mortgage Partners, we're with you for the life of your mortgage: every renewal, every refinance, and any question along the way

## How is that different from a bank?

When you go to your bank, you're working with one lender, and they can only offer their own mortgages. That works well for lots of people, especially if you're happy with your bank and your situation is straightforward.

A broker gives you a wider view. That can be especially helpful if:

- You're buying for the first time and want someone to walk you through it
- You're self-employed, on commission, or have income that changes
- Your credit has some bumps
- Your renewal is coming up and you want to compare offers
- You're thinking about refinancing or using your home equity

## How are mortgage brokers licensed in Alberta?

In Alberta, mortgage brokerage professionals must be licensed by the Real Estate Council of Alberta (RECA). RECA sets education standards, screens applicants, and requires licensees to carry errors and omissions insurance.

You can check any mortgage professional's licence using RECA's [**Find a professional**](https://procheck.reca.ca/find-professional/) tool. We'd encourage you to do that with anyone you work with, us included!

## How are mortgage brokers paid?

This is the question everyone wonders about, so here's the straight answer.

For most standard mortgages, the broker is paid a fee by the lender when your mortgage is completed, not by you. In some situations, like certain private or alternative lending, a fee may be charged to the borrower.

Either way, in Alberta, brokerages must tell you how they'll be paid. And a broker can't charge you a fee for arranging your mortgage until a lender has given a written commitment and you've accepted it.

If you ever have questions about how your broker is being paid, ask. A good broker will happily explain.

## What to look for in a broker

- **A valid RECA licence.** Easy to check online.
- **Clear explanations.** You should understand your options, not just be told what to pick.
- **Honesty about trade-offs.** No mortgage is perfect. A good broker tells you the downsides too.
- **No pressure.** You should feel comfortable taking your time.

## Is a broker right for you?

There's no wrong answer. Some people love the simplicity of going to their bank. Others like having someone compare options and guide them through it.
