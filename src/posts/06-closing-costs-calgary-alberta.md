You've saved your down payment. Amazing! But here's something that catches a lot of buyers off guard: your down payment isn't the only money you'll need on closing day.

The good news is that buying in Alberta tends to cost less at closing than in many other provinces. Let's walk through what to expect, so there are no surprises.

## No land transfer tax in Alberta

Let's start with the happy part. Unlike Ontario or British Columbia, Alberta doesn't charge a land transfer tax. That can save Calgary buyers a lot compared to buyers in some other provinces.

Instead, you'll pay registration fees to Alberta Land Titles.

## Alberta Land Titles fees

When you buy, two things get registered with Alberta Land Titles: the transfer of the property into your name, and your mortgage. Each one has a fee.

Since October 20, 2024, both are charged at $5 for every $5,000 of value, plus a $50 base fee for each.

Here's a rough example, for illustration only. On a $500,000 home with a $400,000 mortgage:

- Transfer of land: $50 + $500 = **about $550**
- Mortgage registration: $50 + $400 = **about $450**

Your lawyer will give you the exact amounts.

## Legal fees

In Alberta, a lawyer handles the legal side of your purchase. They review the paperwork, register everything with Land Titles, and make sure the money ends up in the right place.

Legal fees vary from office to office, so it's worth getting a quote or two. Ask whether the quote includes disbursements, which are the extra costs your lawyer pays on your behalf, like registration fees and searches.

## Other common closing costs

- **Home inspection.** Not required, but highly recommended for most homes.
- **Property tax and utility adjustments.** If the seller has prepaid property taxes or utilities past your possession date, you'll reimburse them for your share.
- **Title insurance.** Your lawyer or lender may require it. It protects against certain problems with the property's title.
- **Home insurance.** Your lender will want proof you've got it in place before closing.
- **Moving costs.** Easy to forget when you're focused on the paperwork!

## Buying a brand new home? Ask about GST.

Resale homes generally don't have GST on the purchase price. Newly built homes usually do.

If you're a first-time buyer purchasing a new home, there's good news: the federal First-Time Home Buyers' GST rebate can refund up to 100% of the GST on a new home valued up to $1 million, to a maximum of $50,000. There's a partial rebate for homes between $1 million and $1.5 million. Eligibility rules apply, so check the details with your builder and the Canada Revenue Agency.

## What about mortgage insurance?

If you're putting down less than 20%, you'll have a mortgage insurance premium. Most people add it to their mortgage rather than paying it at closing. And in Alberta, there's no provincial sales tax on that premium. (We cover this in more detail in our [down payment post](/blog/down-payment-mortgage-insurance-calgary).)

## How much should you set aside?

Every purchase is different, so it's best to get real numbers from your lawyer and lender. But setting aside a cushion for closing costs, on top of your down payment, will make closing day a lot less stressful.

Lenders also want to see that you have enough set aside for closing costs, so it's worth planning for early.
