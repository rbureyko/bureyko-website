Being your own boss has a lot of perks. But when it's time to get a mortgage, many self-employed people find the process feels harder than it should.

If that's you, you're not alone, and you're definitely not out of options. It mostly comes down to understanding how lenders look at your income, and getting your paperwork ready ahead of time.

## Why it can feel harder

When someone has a regular salary, a lender can look at a pay stub and an employment letter and see a steady number. When you're self-employed, your income might change from year to year, or season to season.

There's another wrinkle too. Many business owners (wisely!) use legitimate tax deductions to lower their taxable income. That's great at tax time. But lenders often look at the income on your tax return, so a lower number on paper can mean a lower mortgage amount.

## What lenders usually look at

Every lender has its own rules, but most will want to understand:

- **Your income history.** Lenders typically want to see a track record, often a couple of years of self-employment income.
- **How steady your income is.** Is it consistent, growing, or up and down?
- **Your credit.** A strong credit history can make a big difference.
- **Your down payment.** The government's consumer agency notes that lenders may ask self-employed borrowers for a larger down payment, or require mortgage insurance even with 20% down.

## Documents to start gathering

The exact list depends on the lender and how your business is set up, but it often includes:

- Your personal tax returns and Notices of Assessment from the Canada Revenue Agency for the last couple of years
- Proof that your taxes are paid up to date
- Your business registration or articles of incorporation
- If you're incorporated, financial statements for your company
- Business bank statements

You can usually download your Notices of Assessment from your CRA My Account. Getting everything together early can save a lot of back and forth later.

## If you're incorporated

If your business is incorporated, your income might come to you as a salary, dividends, or a mix of both. Some of your company's earnings might stay in the business.

Lenders look at this in different ways, which is exactly why it helps to work with someone who knows which lenders are a good fit for your setup.

## Tips to make it smoother

1. **Plan ahead with your accountant.** If you're thinking about buying in the next year or two, talk to your accountant about how your tax planning might affect your mortgage.
2. **Keep business and personal finances separate.** It makes your income much easier for a lender to understand.
3. **Stay current with CRA.** Owing taxes can be a roadblock for some lenders.
4. **Look after your credit.** Pay on time and keep your card balances low.
5. **Save a bigger down payment if you can.** It can open up more options.

## Different lenders, different options

This is where shopping around really matters. Banks, credit unions, and other lenders all look at self-employed income a little differently. A lender that isn't a fit for one business owner might be a great fit for another.
