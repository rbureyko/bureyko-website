So the envelope showed up. Your mortgage term is ending, and your lender has sent a renewal offer with a rate and a signature line.

It's tempting to sign and get on with your day. But before you do, it's worth taking a little time. Renewal is one of the best chances you'll get to make sure your mortgage still fits your life.

## First, take a breath. You have options.

When your term ends, you can renew with your current lender, or move your mortgage somewhere else. You don't have to stay just because that's where your mortgage is now.

That renewal letter is an offer, not a bill. You're allowed to ask questions, compare, and negotiate.

## What your lender has to tell you

If your mortgage is with a federally regulated lender, like a bank, they must send you a renewal statement at least 21 days before your term ends. If they've decided not to renew your mortgage, they have to tell you at least 21 days ahead too.

Here's something to watch for: if you don't do anything, your mortgage may renew automatically. If that's your lender's plan, the renewal statement will say so. So give it a careful read.

## Start earlier than you think

Twenty-one days isn't much time to shop around. We suggest starting four to six months before your term ends, rather than waiting for the letter to show up.

## Five things to check on your renewal offer

1. **The rate.** How does it compare to what other lenders are offering someone in your situation?
2. **The term.** Is it the length you actually want, or just the one they picked?
3. **Fixed or variable.** Does that choice still suit your budget and comfort level?
4. **Prepayment options.** Can you make extra payments if you get a bonus or a tax refund?
5. **Your payment.** Will it change, and does the new amount work for your budget?

## Can you switch lenders without the stress test?

This is a big one, and the rules have gotten friendlier.

Since November 21, 2024, if you have an uninsured mortgage (usually one where you put down 20% or more), federally regulated lenders aren't expected to stress test you again when you switch at renewal. That applies as long as you're not increasing your loan amount or your amortization.

If you have an insured mortgage, you also don't have to re-qualify under the stress test when you switch lenders at renewal.

Just keep in mind that a new lender will still review your application, and their criteria may be different from your current lender's. If your mortgage has default insurance, let your new lender know.

## Renewal is a great time to check in on your goals

A lot can change in five years. Maybe you've had a raise, a new baby, or a career change. Maybe you're thinking about renovating, or you'd like to pay your mortgage off faster.

Renewal is a natural moment to step back and ask whether your mortgage still fits. If you need to change something bigger, like borrowing more or consolidating debt, that's a refinance rather than a simple renewal. It works a little differently, so it's worth talking through first.
