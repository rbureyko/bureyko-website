When a renewal letter arrives, it's tempting to sign it and move on. Your lender has made it easy, and it feels like one less thing to deal with.

But a lot can change over a mortgage term. Your income, your family, your plans for the home, and the lending market have all moved since you last signed. A renewal is a natural point to check that your mortgage still fits.

## Start with the offer itself

Look at your renewal date and what your current lender is offering. Note the rate, the term length, and any changes to your payment. Keep the letter handy. It's the most useful document to bring to a conversation about your options.

## Think about what has changed

Ask yourself a few questions:

- Has your income changed, up or down?
- Have you taken on new debts, or paid some off?
- Are you planning a move, a renovation, or a larger purchase in the next few years?
- Has your household changed?

Any of these can affect which mortgage features matter most to you.

## Look beyond the rate

Rate matters, but it isn't the only thing. Payment flexibility, prepayment options, and how easy it is to make changes mid-term can matter just as much, especially if your plans aren't settled.

## Know what switching involves

Moving to a different lender at renewal can be an option. It may involve qualification requirements and some costs. Sometimes staying with your current lender makes sense; sometimes another option deserves a closer look. The point is to compare before you decide.

## Give yourself time

The best time to start is a few months before your renewal date. That leaves room to compare options without feeling rushed.
