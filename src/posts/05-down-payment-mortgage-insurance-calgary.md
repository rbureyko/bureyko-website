"How much do I need to save?" It's one of the first questions people ask us, and for good reason. Your down payment shapes your whole mortgage. So let's break it down, nice and simple.

## The minimum down payment

Your down payment is the part of the purchase price you pay up front. Your mortgage covers the rest. In Canada, the minimum depends on the price of the home:

| Purchase price | Minimum down payment |
| --- | --- |
| $500,000 or less | 5% of the price |
| $500,000 to $1.5 million | 5% of the first $500,000, plus 10% of anything above that |
| $1.5 million or more | 20% of the price |

A couple of examples, for illustration only:

- On a **$400,000** condo, the minimum is 5%, or **$20,000**.
- On a **$600,000** home, it's 5% of $500,000 ($25,000) plus 10% of the remaining $100,000 ($10,000), for **$35,000** in total.

These are minimums. Lenders can ask for more, for example if you're self-employed or your credit needs some work.

## Where your down payment can come from

Normally, the minimum down payment needs to come from your own money. Savings are the most common source, but first-time buyers have a couple of helpful tools:

- **First Home Savings Account (FHSA):** save tax-free for your first home. You can contribute $8,000 in the year you open your first one, up to $40,000 in total.
- **Home Buyers' Plan (HBP):** withdraw up to $60,000 from your RRSP toward your first home, then pay it back to your RRSP over time.

Each has its own eligibility rules, so check with your financial institution or a tax professional before you use them.

## What is mortgage default insurance?

If your down payment is less than 20%, you'll typically need mortgage default insurance. People often call it "CMHC insurance," though it can also come from Sagen or Canada Guaranty.

Here's the part that surprises people: **this insurance protects the lender, not you.** What it does for you is make it possible to buy with less than 20% down.

## What does it cost?

The insurance premium is a percentage of your mortgage amount. The less you put down, the higher the percentage. Here are CMHC's current premiums for the most common down payments:

| Down payment | Premium (% of mortgage) |
| --- | --- |
| 15% to 19.99% | 2.80% |
| 10% to 14.99% | 3.10% |
| 5% to 9.99% | 4.00% |

For illustration only: on a $500,000 home with 5% down ($25,000), your mortgage would be $475,000. At 4.00%, the premium would be $19,000.

Most people add the premium to their mortgage, though you can also pay it up front. If you add it on, you'll pay interest on it at your mortgage rate. And good news for Albertans: unlike in some provinces, there's no provincial sales tax on the premium here.

## Is it worth putting down 20%?

It depends. Putting down 20% or more means no insurance premium and a smaller mortgage. But saving that much can take years, and some people would rather get into the market sooner.

There's no single right answer. It comes down to your savings, your budget, and your plans. That's a conversation worth having before you decide.
