When people think about getting a mortgage, they usually think about their income and their down payment. Those matter, of course. But there's another piece that quietly shapes a lot of the outcome: your credit.

Here at Bureyko Mortgage Partners, we like to say we don't just understand mortgages; we understand credit. So let's talk about it.

## What lenders are actually looking at

Your **credit report** is a summary of how you've used credit over time: credit cards, car loans, lines of credit, and so on. Your **credit score** is a number that comes from that report. It shows lenders how likely you are to repay what you borrow.

When you apply for a mortgage, a lender looks at both. A stronger credit history can help you get approved and may help you get a better interest rate. And if your credit needs some work, a lender may ask for a larger down payment.

## How to check your credit for free

Canada has two main credit bureaus: **Equifax** and **TransUnion**. You can get your credit report from both of them for free, online or by mail.

And here's the good news: checking your own credit doesn't hurt your score. So there's no reason not to take a look.

## What helps your credit (and what hurts it)

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada points to a few key things:

- **Paying on time.** Your payment history is the most important part of your score. If money's tight, making at least the minimum payment helps.
- **Not maxing out your cards.** Try to use less than 30% of your total credit limit. For example, if your card limit is $5,000, aim to keep your balance under $1,500.
- **Keeping older accounts open.** A longer credit history helps. Closing an old card you don't use can actually lower your score.
- **Not applying for too much credit at once.** Lots of applications close together can make lenders nervous.
- **Having a mix of credit.** Managing more than one type of credit well, like a card and a car loan, shows lenders you can handle it.

## A tip for mortgage shopping

When a lender checks your credit for a mortgage application, it's recorded on your report. But if you're comparing mortgage quotes from different lenders, try to do it within about two weeks. The credit bureaus treat those checks as a single inquiry.

Working with a mortgage broker can help here too, since one application can be used to compare options across several lenders.

## Found a mistake on your report?

It happens more often than you'd think. An account that isn't yours, a payment marked late that wasn't, or an old debt that should have dropped off.

If you spot something wrong, you can contact the credit bureau to dispute it. It's worth doing well before you apply for a mortgage, since fixes can take some time.

## Give yourself a runway

If you're planning to buy in the next year or so, now is a great time to look at your credit. Small changes, like paying down a card balance or setting up automatic payments, can make a real difference over a few months.
